CORE: Two-dimensional particle-in-cell runs with a spin-dependent deuterium-tritium fusion module ask whether filamentation can move energy from counter-streaming electrons to prepolarized fuel ions. Magnetic filaments, plus inductive and charge-separation electric fields, accelerate deuterons and tritons that started at rest up to energies where fusion reactions occur. Over the range they scanned, the calculated neutron yield rises with the saturated magnetic-field energy. The model also gives anisotropic, spin-resolved neutron emission, with the strongest polarization roughly perpendicular to the main collision direction.
