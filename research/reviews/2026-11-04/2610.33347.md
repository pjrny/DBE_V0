CORE: Color-dependent syndrome schedules were meant to avoid hook errors that shortcut one anyon type. This paper identifies junction hook errors, where the two data qubits of the hook end anyon strings of two colors at the junction of a logical operator. For distances 7, 9, and 11, every non-corner plaquette is reported to admit a scheduled hook that, with d-2 data faults, makes an undetectable logical of weight d-1. The authors say flags are still required on every stabilizer, including the bulk, if this schedule is to recover full circuit distance. A minimum-weight logical has at most one junction, which they use to explain a remaining distance of d minus floor((d+3)/6) rather than d/2.
