CORE: Relay-BP and beam search, built for independent noise, are tested on spatially correlated noise that keeps the same single-site error probabilities, using a hidden Gaussian Markov random field. Across the simulated circuits the abstract says correlations can change logical error rates by orders of magnitude, shrink the finite-size distance advantage, and reorder Relay-BP, beam search, and BP-OSD. On one of two tested circuits Relay-BP and beam search fall behind BP-OSD, and more relay memory or beam width does not close the gap. At matched error weight, correlated errors cause decoding failures several times more often than i.i.d. errors. Giving the decoders the marginals conditioned on the hidden field recovers most of the loss in the tested settings.
