CORE: For any region and any Pauli noise, including correlated noise, an observer's optimal advantage on a toric-code logical bit is exactly 1 minus 2 P_fail, where P_fail is maximum-likelihood decoding confined to that region. A region that misses one column learns nothing. A spanning region is protected by noise only at impractical distances; the abstract gives d greater than 350 for one row at a flip rate of 10^-3. Under i.i.d. noise a two-row strip learns as little as one row, while below threshold the required width grows only as ln d. The strip threshold is identified with the Nishimori point and located to within a few thousandths by a transfer matrix up to width 16. On two IBM Heron processors at d = 3 and d = 5, an emulated strip adversary shows the predicted jump at width three.
