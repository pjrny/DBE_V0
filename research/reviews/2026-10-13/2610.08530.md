CORE: Experiments at the Orion laser facility follow the temperature of a weakly collisional high-beta plasma whose evolution is mainly sensitive to conduction along an initially quasi-laminar magnetic field. Temperature, density, and magnetic field are read with x-ray spectroscopy, x-ray imaging, and proton imaging. After stochastic magnetic fluctuations grow, the measured temperature history requires the parallel thermal conductivity to be suppressed by at least an order of magnitude relative to the classical Coulomb value. The abstract calls this the first laboratory evidence linking magnetic microturbulence to that suppression. The plasmas it names as the same regime include intracluster gas and inertial-confinement hot spots.
